# Lab 07 — Comparable-Company Policy and Implied Range

## What P/E Measures

The price-to-earnings (P/E) multiple equals price per share divided by earnings per share. Price per share reflects what investors are currently willing to pay for one share, while EPS represents the earnings attributable to each diluted share. Because P/E puts price and earnings on a per-share basis, it helps compare companies of different sizes.

Comparable-company P/E analysis adds a market-based reference alongside a DCF. A DCF depends on forecasts and assumptions about future cash flows, while a comparable analysis shows how the market values similar businesses relative to current earnings. A lower P/E does not automatically mean a company is a better investment because the lower multiple may reflect weaker expected growth or greater risk. P/E can also be misleading when earnings are negative or unusual, or when companies have materially different growth and risk profiles.

## Peer Decisions

### AutoNation

**Decision: Use.** AutoNation is a reasonable peer because its business is comparable to Asbury's in the areas that matter for this analysis: franchised vehicle retail and service/parts operations. Those shared sources of revenue and earnings make AutoNation's P/E useful as a market reference for Asbury, rather than including it merely because both companies operate in the broad automotive industry.

### Group 1 Automotive

**Decision: Use, with qualification.** Group 1 is also a reasonable peer because it participates in franchised vehicle retail and service/parts, which gives it a similar operating foundation to Asbury. Its multiple should still be qualified because differences in dealership mix and the relative contribution of vehicle sales and service/parts could affect growth, risk, and profitability. Those differences could justify a different P/E even when the overall business models are comparable.

## Asbury Implied Valuation

AutoNation's P/E is 10.037825x and Group 1 Automotive's P/E is 11.450149x. The median peer P/E is 10.743987x. Applying these multiples to Asbury's EPS produces an implied price range of $215.81 to $246.18, with a median implied price of $231.00.

The range shows the values implied by the lowest and highest peer multiples. The median estimate is the midpoint of the two peer P/E multiples applied to Asbury's EPS, so it provides a central reference rather than a guaranteed value.

## Leave-One-Peer-Out Result

Removing AutoNation leaves Group 1's higher multiple and produces a median-implied price of $246.18, which is $15.18 above the full-peer estimate. Removing Group 1 leaves AutoNation's lower multiple and produces a median-implied price of $215.81, which is $15.18 below the full-peer estimate.

Removing the higher-multiple peer lowers the implied valuation because the remaining calculation uses only the lower P/E. In either leave-one-out case, only one peer remains. That result should be treated as a reference estimate, not a valuation range, because one multiple cannot provide a peer distribution or meaningful high and low endpoints.

## Reflection

Comparable-company valuation is sensitive to peer selection because each included multiple affects the estimated range and midpoint. This case shows that clearly: removing either peer changes the implied price by $15.18. The result does not prove that Asbury is fairly valued because the calculation reflects the selected peers, their current earnings, and the market multiples assigned to them. It is best used as a valuation reference alongside other methods, including a DCF, rather than as definitive proof of intrinsic value.
